The two dates that decide whether you get paid

Claims bar date. Shortly after the petition, the court issues a notice fixing the deadline — the bar date — by which creditors must file a proof of claim, usually through the claims agent's portal. Miss it and your receivable may be barred from the payout. The date exists only inside that notice, which is why monitoring the docket (or being on the service list) matters more than any rumor.

341 meeting of creditors. The same notice schedules the meeting of creditors under section 341. Attendance is not mandatory, but it confirms the case is live, and the debtor is questioned under oath — useful intel on whether your invoices will see money.

FAQ

What is a claims bar date in Chapter 11?
The court-set deadline, stated in the clerk's or claims agent's notice, by which a supplier must file a proof of claim to share in distributions. It is not a fixed number of days you can memorize — it varies by case, so the notice itself is the source of truth.
What happens at the 341 meeting?
The briefly-named meeting of creditors under 11 U.S.C. §341: the debtor is examined under oath about assets and liabilities. Creditors may attend and question; the date appears on the official notice.
How do I make sure my company gets official notice?
Contact the debtor's claims agent with your invoices and entity details so you are added to the creditor matrix. Until then, docket watching is your only systematic trigger.
Is PACER free?
Not exactly — it is pay-per-page (about $0.10, with low-balance fee waivers), which is why the free CourtListener/RECAP API exists as the self-serve alternative.
