Why these pile up: the arithmetic of one kitchen

A single restaurant renews an estimated 8–15 permits a year — health, fire, liquor, hood/grease, signage — each with its own agency, cycle and fine schedule. Missing one costs $500–2,000 in typical late penalties, plus the closure risk above. That is why licensing expediters can charge $1,000–5,000 per filing and still run waitlists, and why dedicated renewal trackers (e.g. Renewal IQ at $99/mo for the first location plus $39 per additional store) find buyers.

FAQ

How early must Colorado food businesses renew licenses?
Colorado requires the renewal to be filed 45 days before the license expires — file later and the documented penalty is $500.
What is the biggest documented single penalty?
Durango Beer & Ice Co. in Durango, CO: $5,378 in penalties plus roughly two weeks barred from selling alcohol, after its liquor license lapsed and alcohol was sold unlicensed for nearly a year (Durango Herald).
How many renewals does one restaurant face per year?
On the order of 8–15: health, fire, liquor and grease/hood permits run on separate cycles with separate agencies — which is exactly why deadlines get dropped.
What does a licensing expediter cost?
About $1,000–5,000 per filing, and many run waitlists — the market's answer to owners who cannot track filings in-house.
